Rookie congressmen being briefed
WASHINGTON (AP)
	"Training camp" for the three new members of the congressional delegation from New Jersey and 73 other House "rookies" begins this week in Washington.
	Republicans Chris Smith of Old Bridge and Margaret Roukema of Ridgewood, who ousted Democratic incumbents, have been invited to an orientation session for Republicans starting tomorrow at a hotel near Dulles Airport.
	The new Democrat in the 15 member state delegation, Bernard Dwyer of Edison, is invited to a session for the Democrats starting Thursday at the Rayburn House Office Building.
	The sessions, being conducted by staffers of the House Administration Committee, will be followed this Saturday by additional training sessions for members of both parties.
	The new lawmakers, elected Nov. 4, take office in January. The lessons will focus on how to set up a congressional office, what machinery is available, what personnel are needed, how to make use of a congressional expense allowance.
	The new lawmakers will also hear explanations of House rules, procedures and customs as well as House organization and committee structure.
	In addition, a number of groups such as the Conservative Caucus will be holding sessions for the newcomers.
	"I've been invited to a number of overlapping receptions and classes," Smith said.
	Smith, 27, defeated 13-term veteran Rep. Frank Thompson Jr., a Democrat indicted in the Abscam investigation, while Mrs. Roukema, 51, beat Rep. Andrew Maguire, an outspoken Democratic liberal who was trying for a fourth term.
	Dwyer, 59, the state Senate majority leader won the seat being vacated by the retirement of Rep. Edward Patten, a Democrat from Perth Amboy. While the newcomers try to get their bearings in the city where they will spend much of their time over the next two years, their predecessors are packing it in.
	Thompson, 62, has been unable to attend any lame-duck sessions because of his federal trial in Brooklyn, N.Y., on bribery and conspiracy charges. The trial is expected to go to a jury this week.
	In his absence, an aide said staffers have been packing his papers in boxes and crates. Thompson has, for several years, sent his personal papers to a Princeton library, and this year plans to send any excess publications to the city library in Trenton.

	Maguire, 41, said he is pondering several job offers in the private sector, but declined to be more specific.
	An aide said his papers will probably be stored for the time being in the basements of some of those who supported the congressman's campaign.
	Patten, 75, has not disclosed his plans, and his papers are being placed in boxes for delivery to a library at Rutgers.
