California killer stalks hikers
	Calif. (AP) - The discovery of four apparent homicide victims in a remote Northern California seashore park suggests a link with the unsolved murders. of three women in the last 15 months, the sheriff's department said.
	The FBI also is investigating because the area where the bodies were found is National Park Service land.
	Two bodies found by searchers Saturday were those of women hikers. The other two were so badly decomposed that not even their sex could immediately be determined, authorities said.
	Officials said the hikers had been shot to death about 24 hours after they were reported missing. The bodies were found in a wooded area, about 100 feet apart, by Point Reyes National Seashore rangers.
	The decomposed bodies were found about 1,500 yards away, just off the 2.5-mile Sky Trail. The seashore park, 30 miles north of San Francisco, is an area admired for its wild, wildswept beauty.
	Searchers from Marin, Sonoma and San Mateo counties had joined the hunt after friends of the two hikers reported them missing on Friday. The searchers checked the park's forested hills with dogs and horses.
	The identities of the two shooting victims were withheld until their relatives could be notified. But National Park Service spokesman David Pugh did say that one of the dead women was a 22-year-old New York native, and the other was a 25-year-old Idaho resident. They had been hiking separately, and there was no indication they knew each other, he said.
	Marin County Sheriff Al Howenstein said Sunday that the two decomposed victims apparently had been dead five or six months.
	He said he assumed all were murdered. The cause of death was not determined for the two decomposed bodies. Autopsies were planned.
	The sheriff said the evidence suggested a possible link with unsolved murders of three women on Mount Tamalpais trails since the fall of 1979. Talmalpais is a few miles southeast of the park.
	"In all cases, there have been women alone on trails," he said.
	The two female hikers were reported missing Friday. The 22- year-old's disappearance was reported by friends she met along the trail, while the 25-year-old failed to show up for a rendezvous with a friend at Olema, a village at the trailhead, Pugh said.
	Rangers found the decomposed bodies off the Sky Trail, a path on the south side of Mount Wittenberg. Then they found the hikers' bodies.
	Howenstein said the decomposed bodies were clad in shirts and jeans, and he wasn't sure whether they too had been hikers.
	After the Mount Tamalpais killings, alarmed residents began putting locks on their doors and stopped walking and jogging in the woods. The same may happen at Point Reyes.
	Investigators have spent thousands of man-hours in a futile search for a suspect in the Mt. Tamalpais slayings.
	On Aug. 20, 1979, Edda Kane of Mill Valley was found shot to death near a popular trail on Mount Tamalpais. In March, Barbara Schwartz, also of Mill Valley, was stabbed to death on a nearby trail. In October, Anne Alderson of San Rafael was shot to death nearby.
