State lawmen investigating DEP officials - DEP corruption possible
	TRENTON (AP) - State law enforcement officials said they are investigating possible corruption on the part of Department of Environmental Protection officials involved in the cleanup of hazardous waste at the Chemical Control Corp. site in Elizabeth.
	An internal State Police report on the illegal dumping and a fire that erupted in April during the cleanup was revealed in a report published Sunday in The Star-Ledger of Newark.
	References to the State Police report were made at a New York legislative probe of toxic waste dumping last week, when one witness said the report indicated that the fire was caused by arson.
	But Edwin Stier, director of the Division of Criminal Justice, said the report is ongoing and has not determined that the fire was caused by arson.
	He did say, however, that the fire was believed to be "suspicious" and that "certain allegations with respect to DEP are under investigation by this division."
	Stier emphasized that the DEP as a whole is not under investigation, only "certain individuals who have had an enforcement responsibility in DEP and individuals in the toxic waste industry."
	There were indications in the report that some of the persons are no longer employed by the DEP.
	Stier said his division and state police are also investigating safety requirements at Chemical Control, which may not have been properly monitored by local agencies.
	Law enforcement officials are also investigating the relationship between Chemical Control and Stier's division prior to February 1979 when a court order directed DEP to take charge of the company.
	Coastal Services Inc., the firm hired by the DEP to clean up the site and several other toxic waste dumps in the state, is also under investigation, Stier said.
