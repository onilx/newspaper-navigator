School teacher saved by firemen in earthquake
	AVELLINO, Italy (AP) - "The time stood still ... yet I could feel the warmth was slowly disappearing from my mother's body ... it turned cold, icy," said Liberata Sessa of her four-day ordeal under the ruins of an earthquake-demolished home.
	Her mother died but Miss Sessa, a 28-year-old school teacher in Senerchia near Salerno, was rescued by firefighters and regained consciousness almost 24 hours later. She was one of the nearly 60 "miracle survivors" found in the rubble days after the earthquake that killed more than 3,00 people in southern Italy a week ago.
	"My mother and I were chatting in front of the fireplace," Miss Sessa told an Italian interviewer from her hospital bed. "Suddenly the furniture began shaking violently and the whole thing came down on us. It happened so fast neither of us could even scream.
	"I felt as if we had fallen into an underground trap. My mother landed on me in such a way that my arms were around her. From that moment the time stood still. I tried to encourage my mother, I wanted to comfort her. Hearing voices from afar, I said, "Listen, they are arriving, coming to save us."
	At a hospital in Calabrito, pensioner Nicole Mattia, weak and emaciated after six days and six nights beneath piles of debris, told reporters from Naples daily Il Mattino and a radio station that he survived by scooping a small hole through the rubble.
	It enabled him to breath and allowed water to seep through. "I lost a sense of time ... but I kept digging and digging," he said. "I was probably luckier than others because I could move around a little and had a pair of gloves. When I didn't dig, I prayed. All I remember was digging and praying."

	Mattia was discovered Saturday by French technicans working with sophisticated listening devices in search of sounds of breathing, heartbeats, or other noise to indicate buried survivors.
	Gerardo Albanese was pulled from beneath 12 feet of debris after 72 hours.
	"I had just stepped out of the back door of a friend's house to return home, when the world collapsed on me," said the 18-year-old student from Laviano.

	"I immediately felt immense pain, submerged in dirt. It was completely dark," he said. "I could move only my left leg. I felt there was a bit of space in front of my face but I couldn't move my face because an iron object was pressing down on my neck.
	"I knew help was coming. But it was difficult to keep my spirits up. After a while I got depressed and lost hope. Then I heard voices and I began shouting my lungs out. The voices became nearer, clearer."
