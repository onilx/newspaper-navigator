Knights win opener over American, 75-62

Troy shuts down Eagles' Bowers
as Griffin sparks Scarlet offense

By RONN LEVINE
Senior Writer
Riddle of the day: If Kelvin
Troy, Darius Griffin, Roy Hinson,
and Rich Brunson - the main
perpetrators of Rutgers' 75-62
opening night thwarting of the
American Eagles at the RAC
Saturday - played for that same
American team, what would be
their nicknames?
Answer: The Bald Eagles.
But as it is, the Scarlet quartet,
all sporting new Telly Savalas
hairstyles, made American look
like the endangered species en
route to RU coach Tom Young's
second lifetime success against his
former school. The first came ear-
ly in the 1975-76 undefeated year.
Could this be deja vu?
"It was a very, very encouraging
win," said Young, who won 61
games at American between 1969
and '73. "I don't think we ever
played that well defensively."
The main Eagle who had his
wings clipped by the tremendous
Knight pressure was Russell
"Boo" Bowers, college basket-
ball's leading returning scorer
(26.9) this year. Troy's overplay-
ing, ball-hawking defense scared
Boo into only six first half points,
and by the time Bowers got going
(16 in the second half), extinction

time had already passed for the
Eagles.
"I knew he was a great player,
but I just worked hard trying to
deny him the ball," said Troy, who
added a team-high 20 on the other
end.
The player who was not to be
denied was Griffin. The 6-2 junior
guard scored 19 points on seven for
11 shooting, 11 in the second
stanza when he blazed out of the
lockerroom with two steals and
three quick baskets, enlarging a
39-30 Scarlet halftime lead to 47-
32.
"Darius played the best game of
his career," praised Young. "Matt
(Madlinger) played good in the
first half, so Darius had to be do-
ing well to stay out there the whole
second half."
The Knights forged ahead right
at the start and were never headed.
An eight-point burst led by
Hinson's four points (he finished
with 14) gave the Scarlet a 14-4 ad-
vantage. American fought back to
trail by one at 21-20, but four
points by Griffin and two by
Hinson boosted the RU margin
back to seven and the lead never
sunk below five the rest of the con-
test.
"The first half was basically
good for us," said Hinson who pul-
led down a game-high nine

rebounds. "For me, I feel more
confident and more aggressive
than last year. I also worked on
my shooting a lot."
It shows. "Rammin' Roy".
showed a nice touch from the foul
line (six-eight) as did the whole
team (21-26). It is a new feature of
Rutgers, a team usually known for
its poor foul shooting. But there
are several new features to this edi-
tion of Scarlet basketball. The
most prominent seems to be, for
the moment, an under-control
Griffin.
"I worked on keeping myself
more in control," said Griffin one-
half of Rutgers-Lakewood Con-
nection (Troy is the other). "I was
psyched up for this one and wanted
to play well. The guy guarding me
was talkin' a lot, too. If you come
into someone else's building
boasting like that, you better be
able to back it up."
That man guarding Griffin was
named Robin Hooey and he had
two points. So Knight fans may be
hoping that all the guards Griffin
faces this year have as big a
mouth.
The Knights, at times, looked
ready to devour the Eagles in the
second half, but the smaller
American team (their center
stands only 6-6) kept crawling
back.
After building its biggest margin
at 55-38, Rutgers had to hold its
congradulations in limbo as
American drew within seven with
:57 to go. But two Griffin free
throws and four by Troy sealed the
triumph for Rutgers, which went
the final 4:15 without a field goal.
