Letters - RU integrity under fire

To the Editor:
To President Bloustein,
It is becoming more and more
difficult to maintain any
semblance of sanity in light of re-
cent University actions and
policies. Your administration has
consistently shown itself to be in-
sensitive to student needs. (Ad-
ministrative Services). University
policy of late has been at best con-
fusing and contradictory (Baskets
and goalposts). However insult has
been added to injury in the out-
come of the Lonnie Johnson affair.
The University's action is a clear
attack upon student intelligence.
Obviously, Mr. President, you feel
that we students are just plain
dumb.
But I'm sorry, Mr. President,
none of us are dumb enough to
miss the implications in this affair.
If Associate Provost John
Salapatas and the University felt
that they had legitimate reasons
for firing Johnson, why did they
avoid the trial? This surrender
weakens the University's argu-
ment and smacks of censorship. At
this point Mr. President, one can
only infer that the University is in
the practice of limiting freedom of
speech as well as restricting
academic freedom.
One must also begin to question
the integrity of the University Mr.
President. It is ludicrous to think
that no further details than those
already released will be made
public. The University's attempt to
keep the student body in the dark
concerning the settlement is
shameful and outrageous. If all the
elements of the settlement are in-

deed proper, then the University
should have no objection to mak-
ing the agreement public. I ask
you, Mr. President, is it University
policy to pay for conformity?
More frankly stated, how much
did Lonnie Johnson cost?
Due to lack of information, stu-
dent reaction to this has varied.
The Livingston Medium labelled
this affair as a "fiasco." Black
Voice ran a headline claiming, "-
Johnson scores Settlement Vic-
tory." My reaction is that the
University's actions were nothing
less than scandalous. I am shocked
that you would allow the Univer-
sity to sacrifice principle with the
settlement and they cover the en-
tire mess with silence.
Such as things are, President
Bloustein, you must feel secure
that eventually all concern in this
scandal will blow over. I can only
hope that this will not be the case
and that you will come forth with
the facts and clear the air. Prece-
dent however, leads me to be pes-
simistic. I suspect the air coming
down from Old Queens will reek
for quite some time.

Charles Stevens
RC '81
