(Continued from page 1) BSC sponsors program celebrating Pre-Kwanzaa

Stokley Carmichael, will open the
Pre-Kwanzaa holiday as a guest
speaker tonight (day of unity) at 8
p.m. at the Rutgers Student
Center.
Tomorrow (day of self-
determination) is Children's Day
and a closed program for 100
children from the New Brunswick
area will be held at the Cook Col-
lege Center. Recording artists will
be featured at a program entitled
"The Last Poet" tomorrow night
at 8:30 p.m. at the Lucy Stone
auditorium on the Livingston
campus.
A panel discussion on "The
Struggle of the 80's, Looking
Ahead," will be featured Wednes-

day night (day of collective work
and responsibility) at 8 p.m. in the
Busch Campus Center multipur-
pose room. Speakers will include
professors Ralph Carter, Patricia
Thornton, Frederick Wills, and
Lennox Hinds.
On the day of cooperative
economics - Thursday - a flea
market and steel drum concert will
be featured at the Tillett Great
Hall on the Livingston campus.
The day-long program will run
from 11 a.m. to 5 p.m.
Sabu will be the theme of the
day Friday (day of purpose) and
programs will include African
poetry, dance, music and food.
Sabu' will be held at the Cook Col-
lege Center at 8 p.m.
A candle-light jazz affair and
coffeehouse will be highlighted
Saturday (day of creativity) at 8
p.m. at the Corwin Lounge on the
Douglass campus.
Pre-Kwanzaa festivities will
conclude Sunday (day of faith)
with a unity feast and performance
of the play Seven Shades of Black.
The day's program, which begins
at noon, will be held at the Ledge
on the College Avenue campus.
Pre-Kwanzaa is sponsored by
the BSC in conjunction with the
Amanea Society, One Hundred
Black Men, Busch Minority Af-
fairs Council, Students Afro-
American Society, Black
Women's Association for Black
Unity, the Black Voice/Carta
Boricua and various other student
and college oganizations.
