Princeton students approve fee to support New Jersey PIRG

By NANCY GREENBERG-Senior Writer

Princeton University students could be the first "Ivy Leaguers" to officially recognize and fund a chapter of the Public Interest Research Group (PIRG).

In what Paul MacLardy, a full-time organizer for the New Jersey PIRG, termed "an incredible tur-nout," 65 percent of eligible Princeton students voted Nov. 17 and 18 in the student funding referendum, with 60 percent of those who voted approving the $3 per semester term bill fee.

The organization, the largest on the Princeton campus, is currently negotiating with the institution's board of trustees for the establishment of a PIRG chapter, said MacLardy, who added if the students' action is accepted, the university will number one of the few private colleges to support a PIRG.

The campaign, resulting in "the highest voter turnout at Princeton University for any issue," said MacLardy, included the efforts of over 200 student volunteers and an address on campus by consumer advocate Ralph Nader.

PIRG, an independent, student-run organization, focuses on consumer and environmental issues and conducts studies and surveys, produces pamphlets and providesstudent internships.

The organization is also con- ducting negotiations with the Stockton State College board of trustees, the Stockton students for the first time passing the 20-cent per credit fee on Oct. 13-16.

MacLardy said students at Rider, Glassboro and William Paterson state colleges have expressed an interest in giving PIRG a home on their campuses, while Ramapo State College students already support PIRG on a 20-cent per credit basis.

Student organizations at Rutgers are funded by student activities fees allocated by the colleges' student governments. However, according to a 1973 board of governors policy on the funding of special independent organizations, the University's PIRG chapter is funded mostly through a fee listed as mandatory on the term bill.
