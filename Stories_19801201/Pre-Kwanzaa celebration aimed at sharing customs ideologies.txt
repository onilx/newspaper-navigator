Pre-Kwanzaa celebration aimed at sharing customs, ideologies

By CARMELA VETRI - News Editor

This week's celebration of Pre-
Kwanzaa, the African holiday
which gives thanks for the past
year's blessings and welcomes in
the year, will catch the spirit of
"reaffirming values in the creation
of a new energy for future action."
The concept of Pre-Kwanzaa
was created by the Black Student
Congress and former director of
the Paul Robeson cultural Center,
Lonnie Johnson, to celebrate the
10-year-old holiday which falls
from Dec. 26 through Jan. 1.
Because the University com-
munity will not be together during

the Kwanzaa celebration, the BSC
and other sponsors will present
Pre-Kwanzaa festivities, which are
both educationally stimulating and
culturally enlightening, today
through Sunday.
The third annual Pre-Kwanzaa
is the "concept of sharing our
customs, ideas and our ideologies
and the value and beauty of them
with the entire University com-
munity," said BSC President Ron
Washington.
"Pre-Kwanzaa is in that sense
another vehical towards breaking
down misconceptions and barriers
that stand in the guise of ig-
norance," said Washington.

Kwanzaa is based upon seven
principles which, translated from
Swahili, are unity, self-
determination, collective work and
responsibility, cooperative
economics, purpose, creativity and
faith.
"This year's celebration is more
diverse than it's ever been and it's
involved more people than it ever
has," said Washington. "There has
been a conscious attempt to in-
clude the entire University com-
munity not only in attending the
programs, but in sponsoring
them," he said.
