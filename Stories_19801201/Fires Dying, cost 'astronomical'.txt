Fires Dying, cost 'astronomical'
SAN BERNARDINO, Calif.
(AP) - With four fires dying and seven others defeated, firefighters filtered out of Southern California's smoldering timber and brush yesterday - going home after a battle fought at what one official said was an "astronomical" cost.
	Homeowners wiped out by the firestorms that charred tens of
thousands of acres, destroyed hundreds of homes and took four
lives decided whether to rebuild or move to less fireprone regions.
	"Usually, people build right in the same place again, but I just don't know this time," said Gene Knight of the U.S. Forest Service.
	Authorities said the cost of fighting the fires probably will not be determined until long after they are extinguished.
	But U.S. Forest Service spokesman Bill Pidanick said, "The cost will be astronomical. My mind boggles with the figures
for crews, helicopters, contracting of equipment, fuel, food."
	More than 750,000 gallons of fire retardant were dropped for a cost of $457,000 in retardant alone, he said.
	Foresters worried about after-effects of the blazes were busy yesterday reseeding blackened areas in hopes of building up some ground cover to avert flooding when the overdue winter rains begin.
	The disasterous week of fires, which began last Monday, when 100 mph northeasterly Santa Ana winds swept out of mountain canyons and blackened 140 square miles, destroyed about 320 homes and cabins, and ruined dozens of cars, trailers and outbuildings.
	At the fires' height, some 7,710 firefighters called in from as far away as New York were battling the blazes - the second seige of brushfires in the region in two weeks.
	Gov. Edmund G. Brown Jr.'s office has put the damage toll since Nov. 15 at more than $50 million and over 100,000 acres.
	"It's hard to say just how many firefighters are still out," said Knight. "But we are definitely demobilizing."
	The largest fire still burning - and technically out of control, was the Indian Fire in a section of the Cleveland National Forest.
	That blaze has blackened 29,000 acres, destroyed seven structures and damaged 57 others. It was 85 percent controlled by yesterday. But firefighters were reluctant to say when they might bring it fully under control because of difficulty in battling it in rugged terrain.
	A fire is contained when it is encircled with a fire line. It is con- trolled when it is completely out except for mopping up hot spots.
	Also burning were the 12,000 acre Thunder Fire in the MountBaldy area, 40 miles northeast of Los Angeles, the 6,000-acre Lockwood Fire in the Los Padres National Forest in Ventura County and the 2,317-acre Proctor Fire in the Jamul Mountains of San Diego County.
	Those three fires were virtually contained. They destroyed 23 dwellings and hundreds of thousands of dollars of watershed.
