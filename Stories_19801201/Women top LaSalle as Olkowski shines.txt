Women top LaSalle as Olkowski shines

By JIM BERMAN
Staff Writer
The Lady Knights opened up the 1980-81 campaign in impressive
fashion Saturday, as they chalked up an easy 111-64 victory over LaSalle
at the Rutgers Athletic Center.
Rutgers, ranked fourth in the nation going into the contest, won the
ballgame using its greatest asset, its balanced scoring attack. Seven Lady
Knights were in double figures, paced by junior forward June Olkowski's
20 points. "June had one of her finest games here at Rutgers," said coach
Theresa Grentz. Indeed it was.
Olkowski was literally letter perfect, scoring her 20 points on seven of
seven shooting from the floor while hitting all six of her free throws. And
all this in just 20 minutes of playing time.
Joanne Burke had 17 points (14 in the first half) while All-American
center Kris Kirchner, making her official debut in Scarlet, chipped in 14
points (12 in the second half) and nine rebounds.
The game was actually a tight contest early on. Rutgers had trouble
with its outside shooting, and could not pull away early in the first half.
"Offensively, we had a problem early," said Grentz. "We were too pat-
terned, and we're not a patterned team."
The Lady Knights soon broke out of that pattern. The tenacious press,
a lethal Lady Knight weapon last year, proved to be too much for the
Lady Explorers.
With 11:16 remaining and the Knights up 24-20, Rutgers began to pull
away. The Lady Knights went on one of their patented streaks, combin-
ing a crisp fastbreak with some clutch jumpers, outscoring LaSalle 31-14
to put the contest out of reach.
The second half saw the Lady Knights in total control throughout, as
they executed all phases of their game well.
But the key to the victory was the balanced attack. Grentz made it
quite clear that she will not hesitate to go to her bench, perhaps the
deepest group of "backups" in the nation.
"The kids off the bench did well," said the Scarlet coach who raised
her coaching record at Rutgers to a phenomenal 90-32. "Everyone needs
to get the playing time."
