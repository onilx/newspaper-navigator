Letters - Peculiar portions at Brower Commons

To the Editor:
I am writing this to voice my
opinion on the ignorance of the
Brower Commons administration.
When I went through the serving
line on Nov. 23, I was immediately
stunned by the alluring sight of the
rare, but not non-existent Com-
mon's onion rings. At the same
.time, however, I was apalled by the
sight of their so-called "steak."

After letting several people
through for seconds, I finally made
my request for the onion rings
without the steak, only to be told
that this was not possible. I then
slapped myself to be assured that
this was no nightmare and
proceeded to repeat this audacious
request. Again, I was told that I
could not have the onion rings
without the steak. When I asked
what the cafeteria had to gain by
this policy (I was not going to eat
the steak anyway), this Commons
worker, in blind obedience toward
the authority of her superiors,
coldly ignored me and started serv-
ing other students.
I finally gave up and decided to

obtain my four priceless onion
rings by saying the magic words
"steak and onion rings please."
While I have better things to
worry about than onion rings, I
must question the logic of this
policy. That is why I have come up
with a few suggestions of my own
regarding food conservation. Since
hamburgers and french fries go
together like steak and onion
rings, allow no student to have any
french fries without having had
two hamburgers. Then give him
four french fries. Second, since
bacon and eggs seem to be in-
separable, permit no student to
have one without the other. If he
complains, fill his plate up with the

more expensive item. If he persists,
then shoot him (or better yet give
him a free 19 meal plan).
Finally, a bit more on the prac-
tical side, instead of sending all of
the wasted food to be eaten by
some dumb pig, let the students eat
it. It is a shame that these spoiled
animals have priority over us
human beings. The next thing you
know, we'll be giving them our
napkins and silverware. I am ex-
tremely confident that the ad-
ministration of Brower Commons
will seriously consider my sugges-
tions, and help eliminate this terri-
ble problem of food waste.
Richard Karam
RC '82
