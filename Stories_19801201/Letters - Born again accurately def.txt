Letters - Born again accurately defined

To the Editor:
It seems every so often words
are coined which come to express a
certain idea, and the word becomes
stereotypic. These days the phrase
"born again" is popping up more
and more often. The recent rally in
Trenton against Jerry Falwell and
his "Christian Fundamentalist In-
tolerance," along with Mr.
Shapiro's letter of the 12th have
prompted me to try not so much to
break the sterotype but to explain

my own opinion as a Christian on
the subjects they addressed.
To begin with, what is this
"Fundamentalism?" Going to
Webster's you'll find it's a belief in
the Bible as the inspired word of
God; and it's interpreted literally.
The movement of Fundamen-
talism seems to have been an out-
lash against those who saw the Bi-
ble as another piece of literature.
But the word "Fundamentalism"
seems to have taken on a lot more
these days: right-wingers,
homosexual haters, manipulators.
Yet does this mean that all I do as
a Christian is what all Christians
do? I would hope not. There's a
freedom to express differing opi-
nions as Christians and to as-
sociate any Christian with all the
views of one Christian - Jerry
Falwell - is a misconception.

The rally against Rev. Falwell in
Trenton was to oppose his stands
on ERA, abortion and gay rights. I
must admit I'm ignorant of what
ERA will mean if it is passed and
how things would change. But in
God's eyes everyone is equal (Gal.

3:28), and as long as no woman is
adversely affected by its passage, I
see no reason to oppose it.
Abortion, I feel, has become
more of a convenience than
anything else, and life is too
precious to throw away because of
its inconvenience. The creation of
a new life is beautiful in God's eyes
(Ps. 127:3) and should be in ours
also. I can't say I can understand
the feelings of the pregnant
woman, but if I were the father, I
know I couldn't cancel a mistake
of mine by canceling a life.

Homesexuality is definately a
wrong lifestyle to the born again
Christian, but not because it is just
an immoral thing to do. Even way
back in the Old Testament, the
Lord commanded the Israelites
against such things because He
wanted his people to be holy (Lev.
18:22). Thus the Christian should
shun the sin of homosexuality as
he should shun anything from
murder to evil thoughts towards a
fellow man - because it makes us
unholy before God. The Christian
should hate the sin but not the sin-

ner, since we're all sinners. And
when Jesus died to wipe away our
sins before God, he didn't dis-
criminate among sins. The objec-
tive is to try to remove all in us
that makes us unholy before the
Lord.
The point I'm trying to make is
that not all Christians consider
Taiwan to be the only China.
There are fundamental beliefs
among all Christians, but when the
Bible isn't explicitly for or against
something, we're free to make our
own decision. Christians aren't out
to "get" those people who believe
contrary to what they believe, but I
don't feel they should keep their
mouths shut if they feel what the
person is doing is detrimental to
himself.
I don't presume to have covered
the issues throughly, and I may
have stirred up more feelings than
put at ease - but nobody's
perfect. Understanding that is the
first step to understanding what
this "born again Christian
movement" is all about.
David Arzoumanian
RC '82
