Celebration commemorates Japanese alumni

The more than century-old
cultural tie between Fukui, Japan,
and the University reaches a
climactic turn next month with a
busy, two-day round of events in-
volving several units of the Univer-
sity and the city of New Brunswick
as well.
The activities, which will be held
Friday, Dec. 5, and Saturday, Dec.
6, will commemorate, in part, two
principals of the past: Kusakabe
Taro of Fukui, who was graduated
posthumously from Rutgers in
1870, and William Griffis, a
Rutgers alumnus who had tutored
Kusakabe and devoted a lifetime

to collecting Japanese
memorabilia.
Arriving for the occasion will be
two Japanese delegations, one led
by Mayor Yukio Ohtake of Fukui,
who is making a return visit and
the other by President Tadashi
Igarashi of Fukui University.
Among the many events planned
will be the opening of a special ex-
hibit of rare Japanese materials
from the Griffis Collection, which
will be displayed in the New Jersey
Room of Alexander Library; a
symposium built around the dis-
play; a reception, open to the
public, that will feature a variety of

exotic Japanese food; and a con-
cert of Japanese music.
Dignataries expected to par-
ticipate in the two-day affair in-
clude Ambassador Shotaro
Takahashi, the consul-general of
Japan in New York; New
Brunswick Mayor John Lynch and
University President Edward
Bloustein.
The activities will commence at
noon on Friday, Dec. 5, with the
arrival in New Brunswick of
Mayor Ohtake and his party, who
will be welcomed to the city by
Mayor Lynch.
The two municipal officials will

then lead a small group in a visit to
the historic Willow Grove
Cemetery, located on Morris
Street, where the bodies of
Kusakabe and other Japanese stu-
dents who studied in American
schools in the late 19th century are
buried.
Proceeding to the Henry Guest
House on Livingston Avenue, the
mayors will then formally open a
photographic exhibit, where pic-
tures of contemporary Fukui are
displayed.

That evening, a piano recital of
contemporary Japanese composi-
tions will be presented by Chie
Sato Roden, a graduate student at
the Mason Gross School of the
Arts.
The recital, which is free and
open to the public, will be held at
8:30 p.m. in Kirkpatrick Chapel,
adjacent to Old Queens, on the
College Avenue campus.
Roden, a native Japanese who is
married to Donald Roden, a
member of the Rutgers College

history department, will open the
program with a sonata for piano in
three movements by Akio Yashiro,
followed by a children's song and
fantasy taken from Yoritsune
Matsudaira's piano suite,
"Beautiful Japan."
Following intermission, Roden
will present the first part of a
group of preludes for piano by
Akira Miyoshi, and wind up her
recital with a melodic composi-
tion, "Tapestry for Piano" by
Sukegawa Toshiya, which begins
softly and builds to a striking
crescendo.
The activities of the second day,
Saturday, Dec. 6, will be focused
on the symposium, which will start
at 10 a.m. in the Graduate School
of Library and Information
Studies, adjoining Alexander
Library.
Featured lecturer for the
program will be professor Marius
Jansen of Princeton University, a
leading authority on Japanese
history, who will speak on "The
Meiji Modernizers."
