Opinion - Honoring the Jews, as well

By MARK RICH
I am writing in response to the
recent editorial of Mr. Sebastian
Muccilli entitled "Honoring the
Jews" (Nov. 18). In that editorial,
he sought to affirm his respect for
Jews and Judaism and to dis-
asociate himself from those
"forms of Christianity" which
seek to persuade Jews that
Christianity is "better."

The general sentiments expres-
sed in the editorial were worthy
ones in part, yet I feel constrained
to point out an unfair concession
hidden in their midst.
First, please let me emphasize
that the pillar figures of the Chris-
tian faith honor Jews even as Mr.
Muccilli. Jesus was quick to affirm
that "Salvation is from the Jews,"
and the Apostle Paul (a Jew who
expended his life energies to
declare Jesus as Messiah) said, " ..-
.Israelites, to whom belongs the
adoption as sons and the glory and
the covenants and the giving of the
law ... "
However, these pinnacle voice-
pieces of Christianity take a
significant step beyond the
somewhat ambiguous stand of Mr.
Mucilli. They alleged without
equivocation, by virtue of their
conviction that the promises found
in the Old Testament regarding a
New 'Covenant and coming Mes-
siah had been fulfilled, that indeed,
a "better" situation had arrived ..-
.even so much as that which is
promised is better than the
promise itself.
Why does Mr. Muccilli depict
the term "better," when applied to
Christianity, as improper? The
term "better" is used in the New
Testament "Epistle to the
Hebrews" no fewer than seven
times to emphasize the supremecy
of the New Covenant faith over the
Old. It is the book's leading theme
that the New covenant promised
through the prophet Jeremiah and
the new high priesthood promised

through David in the Psalms is tru-
ly a "better" situation. The writer
of the epistle urges his readers to
consider why God should promise
during the Old Covenant a "New"
Covenant it the Old one was ade-
quate ... and why God should
promise a high priest belonging to
the Melchizedekian order if the
Levitical priesthood was truly ef-
ficacious before God.

Mr. Muccilli is in disagreement,
not with certain "groups of
Christians," but with the New
Testament writers themselves,
when he infers that the New Cove-
nant faith of Christianity should
not be depicted as "better" than
the Old Covenant faith. Such
"diplomacy" is presumptuous
when viewed in light of New Testa-
ment assertions referred to above.
Yet we must hasten to add that
Mr. Muccilli is correct when he
implies that ones attitude must be
one of reverence towards the Jews.
The Apostle Paul severely
reproved any Christians who
might be holding their faith with
pride, saying, "Do not be con-
ceited, but fear."
A balanced attitude is required.
Convinced that they have a solid
basis for believing Jesus to be the
Messiah, Christians must from
their hearts share the good news,
yet with much " ... gentleness and
reverence." (First Peter 3:15)

Mark Rich is a 1978 graduate from
Rutgers with a B.A. in music. He is
