Commuter services unpatronized

By ANITA CHOPRA
Staff Writer
"It is a shame that so many off-
campus students don't know about
the many services that are
available to them. Unless a student
comes to the Ledge, it is very hard
for us to reach him," said John
Fee, President of the Off-Campus
Student Association at the Univer-
sity.
The University gives the as-
sociation $16 every year for each
off-campus student. The money is
taken from student fees, according
to Clarence Shive, Association
Coordinator for the past two
years.
One of the services provided for
students living off-campus is stu-
dent advising. Fee said advisors
are available at the Ledge at least
20 hours per week to help com-
muters with personal, financial, te-

nant, or academic problems.
Rutgers College junior Judy
Shafarman explained how she
became a student advisor.
"We were selected after exten-
sive interviewing by the Associa-
tion and the Dean of Students. We
are paid for our work and new ad-
visors are selected every spring,"
she said.
Each advisor is also in charge of
a certain project which is sub-
sidized by the association. These
projects include the '84 club, a
newsletter, intramurals, ride-
sharing, legal advice, and non-
credit courses.
The association also sponsors
many other projects, and meets at
the Ledge every Sunday to discuss
them. Shive said some of the issues
discussed include the possibility of
running a carnival or a concert and
lending support to the Somerset
Street Association which is plann-
ing a block party in the spring.
The association holds many
events for the commuters at the
Ledge, according to Fee.

We want to make the Ledge a
home away from home and it has
potential to be that," he said.
"Last year we had luncheon
speakers such as Dean Clark and
other deans. We also had band
dances and film festivals that were
quite successful."
Most of the events are held dur-
ing the daytime for commuters
who have free time between clas-
ses.

"It is hard being a commuter
and our program is geared toward
making it easier. We try to give
something more than just a school
day to the off-campus student,"
Fee said.
Concerning the New Brunswick
parking deck, Fee said, the deck
currently "seems quite sufficient
for the commuters at Rutgers but
we are having a few problems.
Some New Brunswick residents
are also parking in the deck and
taking up student space. We're try-
ing to come up with some
solutions."
